# Homepage Design Specification V2 — The Brokerage Advisory

**Status:** Current page-specific design specification  
**Authority:** Derived from `design-system/the-brokerage-advisory/MASTER.md` and `docs/creative-direction-v2.md`. Business/content requirements remain governed by `docs/master-website-brief.md` and `docs/claims-and-compliance-rules.md`.

This document tells the implementation what the homepage should **look and behave like**. It is not a replacement for the master system.

---

# 1. Homepage objective

The homepage is a credibility and orientation experience for prospects who often arrive immediately after cold outreach or referral.

The visitor should quickly understand:

1. what The Brokerage Advisory does
2. what kinds of financing situations it helps assess
3. why diagnosis comes before route selection
4. what the advisory process looks like
5. what the next step is

Desired feeling:

> **Reassured.**

The homepage should feel like a **premium advisory instrument**, not a generic financial-services brochure.

---

# 2. Experience sequence

The page follows this narrative:

> **Recognition → Understanding → Method → Options → Evidence through explanation → Action**

Do not interpret this as a repeated stack of generic sections.

Each chapter must have a distinct visual mechanism.

Recommended sequence:

1. Header
2. Hero
3. Situations / recognition
4. Construction & engineering pathway
5. Advisory process
6. Financing pathways
7. Route comparison
8. Direct vs advisory
9. Repayment calculator teaser
10. FAQ preview
11. Final CTA
12. Footer

---

# 3. Global composition rules

## 3.1 What the page should feel like

The visual rhythm should move between:

> **Impact → recognition → explanation → method → exploration → comparison → argument → utility → resolution → close**

The page should change **shape and information mode**, not simply switch background colours.

## 3.2 What to avoid

Do not build:
- hero + three/four cards + CTA repeated down the page
- a bento-grid homepage
- identical two-column sections for every chapter
- a permanent dark/light alternating stripe
- decorative black bands
- large empty dark areas
- generic line-icon feature grids
- fake dashboard imagery
- oversized counters
- generic stock finance photography

## 3.3 Visual intensity

The strongest visual moments should be:
- Hero
- Construction/project visual
- Financing pathway exploration
- Route comparison
- Direct-vs-advisory black argument
- Final CTA

FAQ should be intentionally quiet.

---

# 4. Header

## Purpose

Establish immediate credibility and orientation.

## Desktop

Use a clear horizontal navigation within the 1200px content grid.

Priorities:
1. wordmark
2. core navigation
3. primary CTA

The header should feel architectural rather than app-like.

Avoid:
- excessive utility links
- pill-navigation
- crowded secondary actions
- permanent hamburger-only desktop navigation

## Mobile

Use:
- wordmark
- accessible menu control
- clear route to primary CTA

The header should not consume excessive viewport height.

## Surface behaviour

The header may adapt to the hero surface.

On an obsidian hero:
- light navigation/wordmark treatment
- gold primary action

On a light mobile hero:
- dark navigation treatment
- gold primary action remains the singular action accent

---

# 5. Hero

## 5.1 Role

The hero must answer:

> **Who are these people and why should I continue?**

The hero is the strongest CHAMBER moment on the page.

## 5.2 Desktop composition

Default composition:

- full-width obsidian surface
- inner 12-column grid
- asymmetric split
- H1 occupying approximately 5–6 columns
- visual mechanism occupying approximately 5–7 columns
- strong off-axis positioning
- generous negative space
- one clearly dominant gold CTA

The composition should not be centred.

## 5.3 Typography

Primary H1:

> **When financing gets complicated, start with the right diagnosis.**

Use the approved display hierarchy from the master design system.

The headline should dominate the first viewport without becoming a poster.

Supporting copy:

> We help businesses understand their financing situation before deciding where and how to approach banks, financiers or capital providers.

Primary CTA:

> **Get a Free Financing Assessment**

Secondary CTA:

> **See How We Work**

Trust line remains attached to the hero, not separated into a trust strip:

> Independent, diagnosis-first advisory. Banks and financiers in our local and overseas network.

## 5.4 Hero visual mechanism

The hero must contain an authored visual mechanism.

Preferred first prototype:

**Abstract financing/cash-flow diagram**

The diagram should show relationships rather than invented figures:

- business/project need
- timing pressure
- information/diagnosis
- route assessment

Visual language:
- fine geometry
- restrained linework
- clear labels
- one meaningful gold live marker
- no dashboard chrome
- no fabricated numbers
- no decorative chartism

A documentary image may be tested as an alternative, but the hero must remain complete without photography.

## 5.5 Mobile hero

Mobile is a deliberate re-composition.

At 320–414px:
- re-wrap the headline intentionally
- stack visual and copy
- simplify the diagram
- preserve the black/gold identity
- maintain generous side gutters

Default test:
- light mobile hero for outdoor legibility

Do not force a dark mobile hero merely to match desktop. The final decision should be based on real-device readability.

# 6. Recognition chapter

## Content

Use the approved situations from the master brief:

- Growth opportunity
- Bank rejection
- Existing financing that may no longer fit
- Complex financing situation

Heading:

> **Not every financing situation is solved by another application.**

## Visual mechanism

Do **not** use four identical cards.

Preferred composition:

### Editorial matrix

Four open entries arranged as a composed 2×2 structure on desktop and a sequential list on mobile.

Each entry has:
- small numeric/order marker
- situation title
- short explanatory text
- contextual link

The number is structural, not decorative.

Use hairlines rather than enclosing each entry in a rounded container.

---

# 7. Construction & engineering chapter

## Role

This is the first major WORKS moment.

The purpose is to demonstrate subject understanding, not simply announce an industry focus.

Heading:

> **Built for businesses where project growth moves faster than cash flow.**

Copy remains governed by the master brief.

CTA:

> **Discuss Your Project**

## Visual mechanism

Preferred:

**Project cash-flow sequence**

> mobilisation → procurement/subcontractor obligations → progress claim → certification → payment

Show visually where timing pressure can emerge.

Do not invent:
- percentages
- financing amounts
- approval timing
- project values

A small amount of documentary photography may sit alongside the diagram where it adds context.

## Desktop

Use an asymmetric composition:
- explanatory copy in a constrained column
- large diagram across the remaining grid
- optional image plate as a secondary layer

## Mobile

Re-draw vertically.

Sequence labels remain readable as text.

No horizontal scrolling.

---

# 8. Advisory process chapter

## Role

Show the method as a coherent system.

Core logic:

> **Assess → Diagnose → Structure → Approach**

The six detailed stages from the master brief sit inside this broader four-part spine.

## Visual mechanism

Preferred:

**Continuous advisory spine**

A horizontal progression on desktop that becomes a vertical sequence on mobile.

Each stage has:
- stage number
- concise label
- one-sentence explanation

Avoid six equal cards.

The line should be structural, not decorative.

Gold can mark the current/active stage while the rest remains neutral.

---

# 9. Financing pathways chapter

## Role

Turn the five service areas into an interesting exploration rather than a service catalogue.

Routes:

- Business Financing
- Trade Financing
- Property Financing
- Project & Private Capital
- Performance Bonds

## Visual mechanism

Preferred:

**Large typographic route index**

Each route occupies a distinct row.

Desktop:
- route name large and dominant
- contextual description offset
- exploration link aligned consistently
- active/hover state reveals a small additional visual or description

Mobile:
- stacked rows
- each route keeps its full label visible
- no hover-dependent information

Do not turn this into five equal cards.

---

# 10. Route comparison chapter

## Role

This is a flagship information-design module.

Heading:

> **Compare possible financing routes at a glance.**

Support:

Different facilities solve different situations. The right route depends on your purpose, cash flow, information, existing obligations and the relevant financier’s assessment.

## Composition

Warm ivory reading ground.

Use:
- obsidian table header
- high-clarity column structure
- generous row spacing
- strong left alignment
- subtle hairlines
- visible qualifier/disclaimer
- contextual CTA beneath

The comparison should feel like an **advisory instrument**, not a spreadsheet pasted into the page.

## Data boundary

Use qualitative content only.

Never introduce:
- rates
- LTV
- amounts
- approval timing
- success claims
- public fees
- preferred-lender claims

## Mobile

Do not squeeze the table into tiny typography.

Use a stacked reading sequence where each financing route preserves:
1. route
2. may be relevant for
3. what usually needs assessing
4. next step

---

# 11. Direct vs advisory chapter

## Role

This is the second major CHAMBER moment.

The point is to explain the **sequence of diagnosis before application**, not claim that going directly to a bank is wrong.

## Surface

Full-width obsidian.

## Composition

Two-column comparison:

**Going Direct**

versus

**With The Brokerage Advisory**

Use neutral language from the master brief.

## Visual rules

- large white heading
- warm secondary copy
- strong column labels
- generous empty space
- one gold action element
- no tick/cross iconography
- no red/green "winner" system
- no visual treatment that suggests direct banking is inferior in itself

The message must be balanced and factual.

---

# 12. Calculator teaser

## Surface

Warm ivory.

## Role

Transition from conceptual explanation into useful utility.

Heading:

> **Estimate a monthly repayment for planning purposes.**

The calculator teaser should invite exploration without pretending the result is a quote.

## Visual treatment

- input preview
- clear numerical typography
- restrained gold action
- visible assumptions
- no giant money number
- disclaimer treated as normal readable content

The full calculator remains gated by the approval requirements in the master brief:
- method
- illustrative rate
- worked test cases
- content owner
- review date

Do not invent those during implementation.

---

# 13. FAQ preview

## Role

Reduce uncertainty before conversion.

Use 5–6 approved questions from the master brief.

## Visual treatment

This is deliberately low intensity.

Use:
- generous vertical spacing
- clean typography
- simple accordion/list interaction
- restrained indicator
- no cards
- no heavy decoration

The FAQ should feel calmer because the preceding sections have been visually active.

---

# 14. Final CTA

## Surface

Return to obsidian.

## Role

This is the emotional close.

Heading:

> **Have a financing situation that is not straightforward?**

Copy and CTA follow the master brief.

Primary CTA:

> **Get a Free Financing Assessment**

## Visual treatment

- very large heading
- strong but calm composition
- one dominant gold action
- no urgency language
- no countdown
- no fake proof
- no decorative luxury treatment

The final CTA should feel like a decisive invitation to start a conversation.

# 15. Footer

Use the shared layout system.

Include:
- canonical brand
- navigation
- phone
- WhatsApp
- email
- address
- legal links
- cookie preferences
- copyright

Walk-in language:

> Walk-in visits are welcome by arrangement.

Do not invent office hours.

---

# 16. Gold usage across the homepage

Gold is a semantic system, not a decoration system.

Primary uses:
- primary CTA
- active route
- diagram active node
- focused/high-attention state

Do not use gold:
- for paragraph text on ivory
- for large backgrounds
- for section headings
- as a generic divider on every section
- to indicate the "best" financing route

A section with no functional need for gold should contain no gold.

---

# 17. Photography rules

Use photography only when it adds context.

Preferred:
- construction/engineering environments
- project mobilisation
- plant/materials
- commercial/industrial operations
- credible working environments

Avoid:
- handshakes
- generic smiling executives
- skylines
- luxury interiors
- aspirational property images
- image walls

No image should imply:
- a client relationship
- transaction volume
- lender partnership
- company scale that cannot be substantiated

---

# 18. Responsive rules

Required QA widths:

> **320 / 375 / 414 / 768 / 1024 / 1440**

At every width:
- no horizontal overflow
- no hidden-overflow workaround
- no clipped headings
- no two-line primary CTA
- touch targets at least 48×48px
- diagrams understandable without horizontal scrolling
- comparison labels preserved
- disclaimers remain readable

At 320px specifically:
- treat typography and table readability as first-class constraints
- inspect every long word/CTA before reducing font size

---

# 19. Motion rules

Motion should make the interface feel alive without becoming a feature.

Allowed:
- route hover/active transitions
- accordion expansion
- diagram state transitions
- button state feedback
- subtle surface transitions

Preferred timing:
- 160–220ms

No:
- scroll-jacking
- perpetual reveal animation
- animated counters
- autoplay hero video by default
- decorative parallax
- bounce/pulse
- motion dependency without approval

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

---

# 20. Implementation acceptance test

Before calling the homepage complete, inspect it in this order:

### First 5 seconds
- Does the brand immediately read as Black & Gold?
- Does it feel like a financing advisory rather than a luxury or fintech brand?
- Is the proposition understandable?
- Does the hero feel intentionally composed rather than template-generated?

### First scroll
- Does the page move from recognition into actual financing subject matter?
- Does the construction section demonstrate knowledge visually?
- Is the visual grammar recognisably its own?

### Mid-page
- Do the pathways feel like exploration rather than a service-card wall?
- Does the comparison module feel authored?
- Does black appear where it carries a meaningful argument?

### Close
- Does the FAQ reduce uncertainty?
- Does the final CTA feel like a natural next step?
- Is gold still rare and meaningful?

### Mobile
- Does the page remain recognisably the same brand while being a deliberate composition?
- Is the hero legible outdoors?
- Are diagrams and comparisons genuinely readable?

---

# 21. Implementation instruction

Build **from this specification and the master design system**, not from the current V1 components.

Existing V1 components may be reused internally only when their behaviour remains valid, but their visual styling and composition must be re-derived.

The target is:

> **A premium digital advisory experience that demonstrates expertise through visual explanation.**

Not:

> a polished version of the current V1 page.
